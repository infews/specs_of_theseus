# Product Planning

![Product Planning](img/1_theseus_product_planning.svg)

**Why:** The team needs one prioritized list of work and a person who owns its order. This is the Pivotal Labs or XP planning loop, with an agent helping to break epics down.

**Who:** 🧑🏾‍⚖️ the product manager leads. 🧑🏻‍🎨 a designer helps set product priorities. 🧞 an agent helps break down epics and suggest an order, and 🧑🏼‍💻 developers vet its work.

**In:** Ideas. Stories rejected at [Acceptance](5-acceptance.md).

**Out:** A prioritized backlog of stories - the ideas vetted and turned into intent.

## How

1. **Product Prioritization** 🧑🏻‍🎨 🧑🏾‍⚖️ — rank ideas into prioritized work. Each epic is a collection of work that is releaseable at once to customers.
2. **Choose Epic** 🧑🏾‍⚖️ — pick the next epic to break down and potentially start. This is primarily a business decision, though all team members can contribute information (ideas, intent, technical need).
3. **Epic Breakdown** 🧑🏾‍⚖️ 🧑🏼‍💻 🧞 — split the epic into small, independent stories, each with acceptance criteria.
4. **Prioritization** 🧑🏾‍⚖️ 🧑🏼‍💻 🧞 — order the stories into the backlog. Rejected stories come back here and are ranked again against all planned work.

When the backlog runs dry, [choosing a story](2-updating-evals.md) sends the team back to choose the next epic.

## Raise an issue

An agent should raise an issue when an epic can't be split into stories that can each be accepted on their own, or when a story's acceptance criteria are too vague to write evals from.
